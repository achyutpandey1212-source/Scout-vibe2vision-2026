# Landing Page

## Purpose

The Landing Page is Scout's first impression.

Its responsibility is not to explain every feature.

Its responsibility is to convince visitors that Scout solves a real problem worth caring about.

The entire experience should feel calm, premium, trustworthy, and focused.

Users should understand Scout within the first few seconds without reading large blocks of text.

The landing page should naturally guide users toward creating an account.

---

# Landing Page Goal

By the time a visitor reaches the bottom of the page, they should believe:

- Scout understands the problem students face.
- Scout genuinely saves them time.
- Scout helps them discover opportunities they would have otherwise missed.
- Scout is trustworthy.
- Scout is worth trying.

---

# Landing Page Structure

## 1. Navigation

Minimal navigation.

Left

- Scout Logo

Right

- About
- GitHub
- Sign In
- Get Started (Primary CTA)

Avoid crowded navigation.

---

## 2. Hero Section

This is the single most important section of the entire website.

### Hero Headline

> **Discover opportunities you would've otherwise missed.**

Alternative approved headlines:

- Your next opportunity is probably already out there.
- Stop searching. Start discovering.
- Discover opportunities before they become yesterday's news.

---

### Supporting Copy

> Scout continuously discovers internships, hackathons, scholarships, fellowships, government programs, and early-career opportunities across the web—then recommends the ones most relevant to you.

The supporting copy should remain concise and easy to scan.

Avoid mentioning implementation details such as AI models, crawlers, or APIs.

---

### Primary CTA

Get Started

---

### Secondary CTA

See How Scout Works

This should smoothly scroll to the explanation section.

---

### Hero Visual

Instead of decorative artwork, showcase the product.

Recommended:

A realistic browser mockup displaying Scout's dashboard.

The dashboard preview should include:

- Featured Match
- Recommended opportunities
- Clean layout
- Premium spacing

The product itself becomes the hero illustration.

# 3. Live Statistics

This section reinforces trust.

Statistics should be connected directly to the backend.

Never hardcode values.

Recommended metrics:

- Active Opportunities
- Sources Covered

Example:

```
327+

Active Opportunities

18

Sources Covered
```

Underneath:

> Continuously updated through Scout's automated discovery pipeline.

These numbers should automatically update as the database grows.

---

# 4. Problem Statement

Introduce the problem before introducing Scout.

Example copy:

> Finding opportunities shouldn't feel like a second job.

Students often jump between LinkedIn, Internshala, Unstop, Wellfound, company career pages, Discord communities, Telegram groups, and countless other websites hoping they haven't missed something important.

Most opportunities already exist.

They're simply scattered across the web.

Scout brings them together in one place so you spend less time searching and more time applying.

This section should feel human rather than promotional.

---

# 5. Traditional Platforms vs Scout

Purpose:

Clarify Scout's positioning.

Scout is **not** competing with opportunity platforms.

Scout complements them.

Suggested comparison:

| Traditional Opportunity Platforms | Scout |
|-----------------------------------|--------|
| Built for organizations to publish opportunities | Built for students to discover them |
| Search across multiple platforms yourself | Discover opportunities from one place |
| Generic listings | Personalized recommendations |
| Endless scrolling | Carefully selected opportunities |
| You do the searching | Scout searches continuously |

Avoid criticizing other platforms.

Instead, explain the different philosophy.

# Landing Page Principles

- Large amounts of whitespace.
- Product-first visuals.
- One dominant CTA.
- Calm typography.
- No marketing jargon.
- No exaggerated claims.
- Honest messaging.
- Premium through simplicity.

# Authentication

## Purpose

Authentication should feel like a continuation of the landing page—not a separate experience.

The objective is simple:

Get users into Scout with as little friction as possible.

Authentication should feel invisible.

---

# Authentication Goal

Reduce hesitation.

Allow users to start using Scout immediately.

Nothing should distract from account creation.

---

# Authentication Layout

Centered authentication card.

No side illustrations.

No decorative graphics.

No feature lists.

Minimal interface.

---

↓
